How to avoid ATM fees

Free networks, cashback at stores, and planning withdrawals that cost you nothing.

Why ATM fees add up

A single out-of-network withdrawal can cost $6 or more once you add the ATM operator's fee and your own bank's fee. If you withdraw cash twice a week, that is over $600 a year — money that should stay in your pocket. The good news is that most ATM fees are completely avoidable.

Use your own bank's ATMs

The simplest rule: only use ATMs owned by your bank. They are free for account holders. Use your bank's app locator before you go out, and plan your cash stops around your bank's branches. If you move to a new area, check whether your bank has branches nearby before you commit.

Join a surcharge-free network

Many banks and credit unions belong to networks like Allpoint, MoneyPass or CO-OP. ATMs in these networks are free for members of participating banks. Check your bank's website to see which network it belongs to, and save the network's locator app on your phone.

Get cashback at the store

When you pay with your debit card at a grocery store, pharmacy or big retailer, ask for cashback at checkout. You can usually get $20 to $100 in cash with no fee at all. This is often the cheapest way to get cash, and it works even where there is no ATM nearby.

Withdraw larger amounts, less often

If you must use a fee-charging ATM, withdraw more cash in one trip instead of several small amounts. One $3.50 fee on a $200 withdrawal is 1.75%; the same fee on a $20 withdrawal is 17.5%. Use our Cash Withdrawal Planner to find the cheapest pattern for your monthly cash needs.

Switch to a fee-free account

Some online banks and credit unions refund all ATM fees, anywhere in the world. If you travel often or live far from your bank's branches, an account with ATM fee rebates can pay for itself many times over. Compare the account's other terms before switching.
