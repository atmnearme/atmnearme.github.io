    | Frequently asked questions

    Quick answers to the most common ATM questions.

    In the US, the average out-of-network ATM fee is around $3–$3.50 charged by the ATM operator, and your own bank may add another $2–$3 on top. Using your own bank's ATM is usually free.
    Search "ATM near me" in Google Maps or Apple Maps, use your bank's app ATM locator, or look for ATMs inside grocery stores, pharmacies and gas stations — many are free to use.
    Most US banks set daily ATM limits between $500 and $1,500, depending on the bank and account type. Check the reference table above or ask your bank — you can often request a temporary increase.
    Yes, if your card is on a major network (Visa, Mastercard). You will usually pay a foreign transaction fee of 1–3% plus the local ATM fee. Always choose to be charged in the local currency, not in dollars.
    Keep the receipt, note the ATM's location, time and amount, and contact your bank right away. Banks can check the machine's records and usually correct the error within a few business days.
    Prefer well-lit ATMs inside bank branches or busy stores. Avoid isolated machines late at night, cover the keypad when entering your PIN, and leave immediately if anything feels wrong.
